# What this means

This is the page shape for one durable term, relationship or rule. Use it as an
outline, not a form to fill.

## Section blueprint

```markdown
## <the term or rule>
- Definition: what it means here
- Applies to: the boundary within which the definition holds
- Related: the neighbouring terms readers confuse with it, and the difference
- Example: one concrete case that shows the definition in use
```

`Definition` is required and comes first. `Applies to` is what separates a
definition from a slogan: the same word often means different things in two
systems, and a definition without its boundary will be applied where it is wrong.
`Related` earns its place only when a real confusion exists.

A page may define one concept or a coherent group of terms and rules needed to
understand it. Give each term a searchable heading. Split independent subjects,
not every term: avoid both unrelated glossary dumps and thin one-term pages.
Group by what readers need together, not the order of a discussion.

Sessions are where terms get pinned down, usually because two people meant
different things by one word. That makes them a strong source for `Related` and
`Applies to` — the disagreement itself shows where the boundary lies. It also
makes them easy to overstate: agreement among participants establishes shared
usage in that context, not an organization-wide standard. If the discussion
settled the meaning for one team or one system, the boundary says so.
